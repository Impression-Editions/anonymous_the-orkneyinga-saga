About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were distinguished scholars whose collaborative efforts brought the rich heritage of the Norse world to a wider English-speaking audience. Goudie, a Scottish antiquary and expert on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival knowledge with Hjaltalín’s linguistic expertise as an Icelandic scholar and librarian. Together, they bridged the gap between the medieval sagas and the Victorian literary tradition, ensuring that the complex narratives of the North were preserved with both historical rigor and narrative grace.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga remains a cornerstone of Old Norse studies, celebrated for its meticulous attention to the genealogical and geographical intricacies of the original text. By rendering these foundational accounts of the Earls of Orkney into accessible prose, Goudie and Hjaltalín provided an essential resource for historians and enthusiasts alike. Their scholarly contributions continue to serve as a vital gateway for readers seeking to understand the turbulent and storied past of the North Sea territories.
