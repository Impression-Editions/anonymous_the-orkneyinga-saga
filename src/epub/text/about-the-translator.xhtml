About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1844⁠–⁠1935) were distinguished scholars whose collaborative efforts were instrumental in bringing the rich heritage of Old Norse literature to a wider English-speaking audience. Goudie, a Scottish antiquary and historian, possessed a deep fascination with the Norse influence on the Northern Isles, while Hjaltalín, an Icelandic scholar and librarian, provided an essential linguistic bridge to the original sagas. Together, they combined rigorous historical research with a profound respect for the cultural nuances of the medieval North.

Their most significant contribution to the field remains their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga, a work that serves as a cornerstone for understanding the history of the Earldom of Orkney. By meticulously rendering the complex narratives of Viking-age chieftains and explorers into accessible prose, Goudie and Hjaltalín preserved the epic spirit of the original text for future generations. Their scholarly dedication not only solidified the saga’s place in the literary canon but also fostered a lasting appreciation for the interconnected history of Scotland and Scandinavia.
