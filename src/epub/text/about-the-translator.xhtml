About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were pioneering scholars whose collaborative efforts were instrumental in bringing the rich heritage of Old Norse literature to an English-speaking audience. Goudie, a distinguished Scottish antiquary and authority on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival expertise with Hjaltalín’s profound linguistic mastery as an Icelandic scholar and librarian. Together, they bridged the cultural divide between the Viking Age and the modern era, meticulously documenting the historical connections between Scotland and Scandinavia.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga remains a cornerstone of medieval scholarship, celebrated for its rigorous fidelity to the original manuscripts and its evocative rendering of the saga’s epic narrative. By illuminating the complex political and social landscape of the Earldom of Orkney, their work provided essential context for the maritime history of the North Atlantic, including the strategic importance of regions such as the Minch. Through their enduring contributions to translation and historical research, Goudie and Hjaltalín ensured that the heroic chronicles of the North would continue to resonate with readers and historians for generations to come.
