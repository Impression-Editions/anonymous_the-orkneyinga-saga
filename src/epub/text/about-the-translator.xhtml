About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1844⁠–⁠1935) were distinguished scholars whose collaborative efforts were instrumental in bringing the rich heritage of Old Norse literature to a wider English-speaking audience. Goudie, a Scottish antiquary and historian with a profound devotion to the history of the Northern Isles, combined his meticulous research skills with Hjaltalín’s deep linguistic expertise as an Icelandic scholar and librarian. Together, they bridged the cultural divide between the medieval sagas and the Victorian literary world, ensuring that the complex narratives of the North were preserved with both historical accuracy and narrative grace.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga remains a cornerstone of their shared legacy, standing as a testament to their commitment to preserving the epic history of the Earls of Orkney. Beyond this seminal work, both men contributed extensively to the field of Scandinavian studies through their scholarly articles and archival research, which shed new light on the interconnected histories of Scotland and Iceland. By rendering these ancient accounts into accessible prose, Goudie and Hjaltalín provided an enduring foundation for modern readers to explore the dramatic, often turbulent, Viking Age history that shaped the North Sea world.
