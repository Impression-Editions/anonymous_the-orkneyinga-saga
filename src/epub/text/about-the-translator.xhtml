About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were pioneering scholars whose collaborative efforts were instrumental in bringing the rich heritage of Old Norse literature to an English-speaking audience. Goudie, a distinguished Scottish antiquary and authority on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival expertise with Hjaltalín’s profound linguistic mastery as an Icelandic scholar and librarian. Together, they bridged the cultural divide between the Viking Age and the modern era, meticulously documenting the historical geography of the North Atlantic, including the treacherous waters of the Minch and the Hebridean straits that once served as vital conduits for Norse expansion.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga remains a cornerstone of medieval scholarship, celebrated for its narrative clarity and rigorous attention to the original Icelandic manuscripts. By rendering these complex sagas into accessible prose, Goudie and Hjaltalín preserved the epic accounts of the Earls of Orkney, ensuring that the legacy of the Norse settlers in Scotland would endure for generations of readers. Their work continues to be regarded as an essential contribution to the study of Scandinavian history, reflecting a lifelong commitment to the preservation of the sagas and the cultural landscapes they describe.
