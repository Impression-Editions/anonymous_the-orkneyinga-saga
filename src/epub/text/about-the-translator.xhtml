About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were pioneering scholars whose collaborative efforts were instrumental in bringing the rich heritage of Old Norse literature to an English-speaking audience. Goudie, a distinguished Scottish antiquary and authority on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival expertise with Hjaltalín’s profound linguistic mastery as an Icelandic scholar and librarian. Together, they bridged the cultural divide between the Viking Age and the modern era, meticulously documenting the historical geography of the North Atlantic, including the treacherous waters of the Minch and the Hebridean straits that once served as vital corridors for Norse expansion.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga remains a cornerstone of medieval scholarship, celebrated for its rigorous fidelity to the original manuscripts and its evocative rendering of the saga’s epic narrative. By illuminating the complex political and social tapestries of the earldom of Orkney, Goudie and Hjaltalín provided readers with an essential window into the lives of the Norsemen who shaped the destiny of the British Isles. Their enduring contribution to the field continues to serve as a foundational text for historians and literary enthusiasts alike, preserving the legacy of the sagas for generations to come.
