About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were distinguished scholars whose collaborative efforts brought the rich heritage of the North Atlantic to a wider audience. Goudie, a Scottish antiquary and expert on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival knowledge with Hjaltalín’s profound linguistic expertise as an Icelandic scholar and librarian. Together, they bridged the cultural divide between the Norse sagas and the English-speaking world, ensuring that the complex narratives of the Viking Age remained accessible and historically grounded.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga stands as a cornerstone of their literary partnership, reflecting a meticulous commitment to preserving the epic tone and genealogical precision of the original text. Beyond this seminal work, both men were prolific contributors to the study of Scandinavian and Scottish history, with Goudie’s extensive research into the antiquities of Shetland and Hjaltalín’s contributions to Icelandic literature cementing their reputations as essential figures in nineteenth-century historiography. Their enduring legacy continues to inform modern scholarship, providing an indispensable window into the medieval world of the North Sea.
