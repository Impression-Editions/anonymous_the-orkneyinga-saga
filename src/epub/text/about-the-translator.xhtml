About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were distinguished scholars whose collaborative efforts brought the rich heritage of the Norse world to a wider English-speaking audience. Goudie, a Scottish antiquary and expert on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival research with the linguistic expertise of Hjaltalín, an Icelandic scholar and librarian at the Advocates Library in Edinburgh. Together, they bridged the gap between the medieval sagas and the Victorian literary tradition, ensuring that the complex narratives of the North remained accessible and historically grounded.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga stands as a cornerstone of their shared legacy, providing a vital window into the lives of the Earls of Orkney and the turbulent history of the Viking Age. Beyond this seminal work, both men were prolific contributors to the study of Northern European history, with Goudie’s extensive writings on the antiquities of Shetland and Hjaltalín’s contributions to Icelandic literature cementing their reputations as preeminent authorities in their respective fields. Their meticulous approach to the text remains a touchstone for modern historians and enthusiasts of Old Norse literature alike.
