About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were pioneering scholars whose collaborative efforts were instrumental in bringing the rich history of the Norse world to an English-speaking audience. Goudie, a distinguished Scottish antiquary and expert on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival knowledge with Hjaltalín’s profound linguistic expertise as an Icelandic scholar and librarian. Together, they bridged the cultural divide between the medieval sagas and modern historical inquiry, ensuring that the complex narratives of the North Atlantic were preserved with both academic rigor and literary sensitivity.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga remains a cornerstone of Old Norse scholarship, celebrated for its clarity and its faithful rendering of the turbulent, heroic spirit of the original text. Beyond this monumental work, both men contributed extensively to the study of Scandinavian influence in Scotland, with Goudie’s research into the runic inscriptions and social history of Orkney and Shetland providing essential context for their translations. By meticulously documenting the connections between the Hebridean straits⁠—such as the Minch, once known as Skotlandsfjörð⁠—and the broader Norse maritime empire, Goudie and Hjaltalín transformed how readers understand the historical landscape of the British Isles.
