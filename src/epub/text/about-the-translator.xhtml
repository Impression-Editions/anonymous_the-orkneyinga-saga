About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1844⁠–⁠1935) were distinguished scholars whose collaborative efforts in the late nineteenth century proved instrumental in bringing the rich heritage of Old Norse literature to an English-speaking audience. Goudie, a Scottish antiquary and historian, possessed a deep-seated passion for the cultural legacy of the Northern Isles, while Hjaltalín, an Icelandic scholar and librarian, provided the essential linguistic expertise required to navigate the complexities of the original sagas. Together, they bridged the gap between the medieval North Atlantic world and the Victorian literary landscape.

Their most significant contribution remains their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga, a seminal work that chronicles the history of the Earls of Orkney and their intricate connections to the Norse kings. By meticulously rendering the saga’s stark, heroic prose into accessible English, Goudie and Hjaltalín preserved the historical integrity and narrative power of this foundational text. Their scholarly rigor and dedication to the source material continue to serve as a cornerstone for modern researchers and enthusiasts of Norse history, ensuring that the epic tales of the North remain vibrant for contemporary readers.
