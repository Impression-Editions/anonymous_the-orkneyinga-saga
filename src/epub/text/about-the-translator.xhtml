About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were pioneering scholars whose collaborative efforts were instrumental in bringing the rich heritage of the Norse world to an English-speaking audience. Goudie, a distinguished Scottish antiquary and authority on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival expertise with Hjaltalín’s profound linguistic mastery of Old Icelandic. Together, they bridged the cultural divide between the Viking Age and the modern era, ensuring that the complex narratives of the North remained accessible to both historians and general readers.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga stands as a cornerstone of their shared literary legacy. By meticulously rendering the epic accounts of the Earls of Orkney, they provided a vital window into the political and social landscape of the medieval North Atlantic. Beyond this landmark work, their scholarly contributions⁠—ranging from the study of runic inscriptions to the preservation of Shetlandic folklore⁠—remain essential references for those exploring the historical intersections of Scotland and Scandinavia. Their enduring commitment to accuracy and narrative clarity continues to inform the study of the sagas today.
