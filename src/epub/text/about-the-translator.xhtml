About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were distinguished scholars whose collaborative efforts brought the rich heritage of the Norse world to a wider English-speaking audience. Goudie, a Scottish antiquary and expert on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival knowledge with Hjaltalín’s linguistic expertise as an Icelandic scholar and librarian. Together, they bridged the gap between the medieval sagas and modern historical inquiry, ensuring that the cultural nuances of the North Atlantic were preserved with both accuracy and narrative grace.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga remains a cornerstone of Old Norse studies, celebrated for its meticulous attention to the complex genealogies and heroic exploits of the Earls of Orkney. Beyond this seminal work, both men contributed extensively to the field of Scandinavian history through their scholarly articles and research into the cultural intersections of Scotland and Iceland. By rendering these ancient accounts into accessible prose, Goudie and Hjaltalín provided an enduring foundation for all who seek to understand the turbulent and storied past of the Viking Age.
