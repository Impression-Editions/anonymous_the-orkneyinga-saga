About the Translator

Gilbert‌​‌​​‌‌‌​​‌‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌​​​​‌‌​‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌​​​‌​​​​​​​​‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌‌‌‌​​‌​​​‌‌‌‌​‌‌​​​‌​​‌​‌​​​​​‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌‌​‌​​​‌​​​​‌​‌‌​​‌​​‌‌‌​​​‌‌​‌​‌​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​‌‌‌‌​​‌​‌‌‌​‌​​‌​​​​‌‌‌​‌​​​​‌​‌​‌​​​​​​‌​‌​​‌‌‌‌​​‌‌​​​‌​​​​​‌‌​​​‌‌‌​‌‌‌​‌​‌​‌‌​​​​‌​‌‌​‌‌​​​​​​​ Goudie (1843⁠–⁠1918) and Jón A. Hjaltalín (1840⁠–⁠1908) were pioneering scholars whose collaborative efforts were instrumental in bringing the rich heritage of Old Norse literature to an English-speaking audience. Goudie, a distinguished Scottish antiquary and authority on the history of the Northern Isles, combined his deep archival expertise with Hjaltalín’s profound linguistic mastery as an Icelandic scholar and librarian. Together, they bridged the cultural divide between the Viking Age and the modern era, meticulously documenting the historical geography of the North Atlantic, including the treacherous waters of the Minch and the Hebridean reaches that feature so prominently in the sagas.

Their definitive translation of The Orkneyinga Saga remains a cornerstone of medieval scholarship, celebrated for its rigorous fidelity to the original manuscripts and its evocative rendering of the Norse world. Beyond this monumental work, both men contributed extensively to the study of Scandinavian influence in Scotland, providing essential context for the political and social landscapes of the medieval period. By illuminating the complex narratives of the Orkney jarls, Goudie and Hjaltalín ensured that these foundational tales of exploration, conflict, and legacy would endure for generations of readers and historians alike.
